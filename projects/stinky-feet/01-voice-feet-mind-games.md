## 3. The structure (your "stinky mindfuck," move by move)

This is the order the moves come in. A short X caption uses 3 of them. A Stinky Feet Report uses all 5.

### Move 1: The ordinary moment

Open on something that actually happened to your feet today. The first 3 to 5 words have to open a gap (your rule from the Voice DNA interview: "I almost didn't film this one" beats "Hey everyone, I made a new video").

- Yours: "Kicked my feet up and called it a night."
- Why it works: it reads like a real woman on her couch, which is the thing your fans told you they want ("we don't want a porn star, we want the real one"). Router Layer 1: real over polished.

### Move 2: Point his eyes

Tell him exactly where to look. One detail only.

- Yours: "Stare at my big feet." · "do you see it..."
- Why it works: you're directing attention instead of describing. He does the looking, so he does the wanting.

### Move 3: Put it in his body

Move the stink from the picture into his nose, throat, or chest.

- Yours: "Feel that pull in your chest..." and from your interview: "steeps into your pores, clings to your skin, goes down your throat, up your nose, wraps around your brain."
- Receipt: in a 2006 fMRI study, people who simply **read** smell words like "garlic" and "cinnamon" showed activity in the brain's primary smell areas (González et al., *NeuroImage* 32(2):906-912). Your smell words do real work in his head. Your sensory-first rule has a brain scan behind it.

### Move 4: Catch him

Say what he's doing right now, as if you're watching.

- Yours: "You think you're just scrolling…but you stopped, didn't you. Caught you."
- Why it works: the post becomes about the two of you. Second person is Router Layer 1.
- Receipt for "your whole day is ruined in the best way": psychologists call this benign masochism. People enjoy experiences their body reads as bad (and disgust is one of the categories they studied) once they know they're safe (Rozin et al. 2013, *Judgment and Decision Making* 8(4):439-447). Stink is disgust turned into pleasure. You name that flip out loud, and it lands.
- The one contrast move you're allowed: "You think X…but you stopped." It works for you because it's a catch about what he's doing this second. Use it once per post at most. "It's not X, it's Y" is still banned (Router Layer 2).

### Move 5: Leave the loop open

End on a question, a choice, or a thought he has to finish himself. Leave it unresolved.

- Yours: "Quick question..." followed by a choice.
- Receipt 1: curiosity peaks when there's a small, specific gap in what someone knows (Loewenstein 1994, *Psychological Bulletin* 116:75-98). Give him 1 concrete detail and hold back the rest.
- Receipt 2: in the ranking code X open-sourced in 2023, a reply was weighted 13.5 and a like 0.5, so one reply counted for about 27 likes. A reply that the author answers was weighted 75. Your choice-question ending is the exact thing the feed rewards, and answering the people who reply multiplies it. Your best post got 43 replies on 514 likes, which is 1 reply per 12 likes.
- One limit: X's own July report shows an adult label on one of your accounts, and that label keeps its posts out of strangers' recommendations. So replies lift you in your followers' feeds, not in front of new people (section 7).
